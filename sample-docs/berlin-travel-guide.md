# Berlin Travel Guide

Berlin is the most interesting city I have visited in Europe, and I say that as
someone who has been three times and keeps finding reasons to go back. It is messy,
creative, affordable by western European standards, and unlike any other capital.
Here is what I wish someone had told me before my first trip.

## Neighbourhoods

**Kreuzberg** is where I spend most of my time. It is the cultural heart of the
city — street art on every block, independent bookshops, vinyl record stores, and
some of the best food in Berlin. The stretch along the Landwehr Canal is perfect
for a slow afternoon walk. If you only visit one neighbourhood, make it this one.

**Mitte** is the historic centre and museum district. Museum Island alone has five
world-class museums, including the Pergamon (ancient artifacts) and the Neues Museum
(the bust of Nefertiti is here). It is the most touristy area, but the sights
genuinely earn the crowds. The Brandenburger Tor and the Holocaust Memorial are
both in Mitte and worth seeing even if you dislike "tourist stuff."

**Prenzlauer Berg** is where you go for brunch. The neighbourhood is leafy, calm,
and full of young families. Helmholtzplatz on a Saturday morning is the vibe —
outdoor seating, flat whites, and sourdough everything. It is also home to the
Mauerpark flea market, which is chaotic and wonderful and best visited before noon.

**Neukolln** comes alive at night. It has the best bar scene in the city — small,
unpretentious spots where a beer costs three euros and the DJ plays from a laptop
on a shelf. Weserstrasse and Sonnenallee are the main strips. It is also the most
diverse neighbourhood in Berlin, which means the food scene is outstanding — Turkish
bakeries, Vietnamese street food, Ethiopian restaurants, all within a few blocks.

## Getting Around

Berlin's public transit is excellent. The U-Bahn (underground) and S-Bahn
(overground rail) cover the entire city, and they run until about 1am on weeknights
and all night on weekends. Buy a day pass (Tageskarte) — it costs around eight
euros and pays for itself after three rides.

Cycling is the other way to move. The city is flat and bike lanes are everywhere.
Rent a bike from one of the dockless rental apps (Lime and Tier are the most
common) or ask your hotel. Some of my best memories are cycling along the Spree
river at sunset with no particular destination.

## Food

You cannot visit Berlin without eating a currywurst at least once. It is a
chopped bratwurst drenched in curry ketchup, served with fries, and consumed
standing at a counter. Curry 36 in Kreuzberg is the famous one; Konnopke's Imbiss
in Prenzlauer Berg is just as good with shorter queues.

The doner kebab in Berlin is a different animal from what you get in most other
cities. The bread is fresher, the meat is better seasoned, and the salad-and-sauce
game is serious. Mustafa's Gemuse Kebap has a legendary queue, but honestly, most
corner kebab shops are excellent. Do not wait 45 minutes — just walk into the
nearest one.

Berlin's third-wave coffee scene has exploded in the last few years. The Barn, Five
Elephant, and Bonanza Coffee are the big names. Expect single-origin pour-overs,
minimalist interiors, and baristas who take their craft seriously. A flat white
runs about four euros.

## Cultural Tips

**Cash is still common.** Many smaller restaurants, bars, and market stalls do not
accept cards. Always carry at least 30 to 50 euros in cash. This is changing
slowly, but you will hit a cash-only spot at least once.

**Sundays are quiet.** Almost all shops are closed on Sundays by law. Supermarkets,
clothing stores, pharmacies — all shut. Restaurants and cafes stay open, but plan
your grocery shopping for Saturday. Train station shops are the exception and stay
open seven days a week.

**Tipping is relaxed.** Round up or add 10 percent. Nobody expects American-style
20 percent tips. If you pay in cash, tell the server the total you want to pay
rather than leaving coins on the table — for example, if the bill is 17 euros, say
"make it 19."
